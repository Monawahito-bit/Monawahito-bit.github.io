CASE STUDY 01 · EXPERIMENT & ANALYSIS

From reactive recovery to proactive activation

An investigation into when learners disengage — and whether intervening earlier can improve the likelihood of recovery.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

The problem: recovery was happening too late

After two months of running self-paced Creative Tech programmes, activation was stuck at 16%. Many paid and enrolled learners had stalled, with some inactive for more than three weeks.

The initial response relied on generic follow-up messages after 21 days of inactivity. That approach was reactive: by the time the message arrived, many learners had already disengaged.

02 / THE HYPOTHESIS

Earlier intervention could improve the chance of recovery

The working hypothesis was that learners who had recently stopped progressing would be more recoverable than learners who had been inactive for a much longer period. Instead of waiting 21 days, the team would intervene earlier and examine recovery by inactivity band.

03 / EXPERIMENT DESIGN

Test the timing of the intervention

The campaign included 5,809 inactive learners. Learners were grouped by how long they had been inactive when the campaign began, allowing the team to compare recovery patterns across inactivity durations.

The intervention trigger was moved forward: learners entered the campaign after 8 days of inactivity rather than waiting for the 21-day inactivity follow-up.

04 / WHAT THE DATA SHOWED

Recovery was concentrated among recently stalled learners

The strongest recovery signal came from learners who had been inactive for approximately 5–12 days. Learners inactive for more than 12 days were substantially less likely to return.

This shifted the operational question from “How do we remind inactive learners?” to “How early must we identify stalled behaviour to make recovery more likely?”

05 / WHAT CHANGED

Move from a late reminder to an earlier recovery trigger

The key operational implication was to act earlier, while learners were more likely to be recoverable. Technical Mentor support also mattered for learners who were locked out of content or needed help understanding concepts.

06 / LEARNINGS & NEXT STEPS

Design interventions around behaviour, not arbitrary waiting periods

  • Recent inactivity can be a more useful intervention signal than a long inactivity threshold.
  • Segmentation matters: learners at different inactivity stages may need different interventions.
  • Messaging alone cannot solve access or concept-support barriers.
