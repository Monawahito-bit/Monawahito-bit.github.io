Designing a community engagement operating model

Redesigning a creative learning community from high-volume activity to a more intentional system for rhythm, accountability and visible progress.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

The problem: activity was not the same as progress

The community experience had become heavily activity-driven, with a high volume of events and engagement touchpoints. But more activity did not automatically translate into learner progression, completion or sustained participation.

The strategic challenge was to redesign the operating model so that community rituals supported the learning journey instead of competing with it.

02 / STRATEGIC RESPONSE

Design the system around three behaviours

From Cohort 9 onwards, the model shifted from activity-chasing to behaviour design. It was organised around three connected pillars:

  • Rhythm: create a predictable, manageable cadence of meaningful touchpoints.
  • Accountability: help learners make commitments and follow through with peer support.
  • Visibility: make progress, contribution and achievement easier to see.

03 / IMPLEMENTATION

Reduce noise and strengthen the moments that matter

Event volume was reduced from approximately 14–18 events to 7 per cohort. The model introduced structured peer momentum through Momentum Pods and Squad Leads, alongside visibility mechanisms such as a Public Diary, leaderboards and Legacy Points.

05 / WHAT WE LEARNED

A community model needs both structure and proof

Reducing event volume is not enough on its own. The operating model must make it easier for learners to know what to do next, see their progress, get support and remain accountable.

06 / NEXT ITERATION

Make the model measurable and repeatable

The next iteration should connect each ritual to a defined learner behaviour and an outcome metric. It should also reduce manual measurement so the team can understand engagement and progression without relying on labour-intensive tracking.
